# Recording guide

How to film an experiment so FrameLab can measure it. These tips come from the recordings made
for this project. A shorter version is in the app under **How to record** (English and Indonesian).

## All experiments

- **Keep the phone still.** Use a tripod or lean it on books. Never hold it.
- **Face the motion squarely.** Put the lens at the height of the middle of the motion, level,
  1.5–2 m from the wall. Let the motion fill about two thirds of the frame, not edge to edge:
  near the edges of the frame the scale is no longer the same. (Our free-fall clip that
  filled the whole frame came out 12 % low; its ball looked larger near the bottom.)
- **Plain background, good light.** A plain wall in a colour that contrasts with the object,
  light from the camera side. More light means less motion blur.
- **Say the numbers at the start**, in English or Indonesian, for example *"Panjang talinya
  empat puluh senti, saya kira periodenya satu setengah detik"*. FrameLab listens with Muse
  Voice Transcribe and uses only values that were actually said. Measure them; do not guess.
- **10–20 seconds per clip.** Let go within a second or two after speaking, and keep your hand
  out of the picture afterwards.
- **Only hands in the picture** if children help.

## Pendulum (recommended on an ordinary phone)

The pendulum gives g from time (the period) and the string length, so it does not need a ruler
in the picture. Our three accepted pendulum runs were within 1.6 % of local gravity.

- A small, heavy bob on a thin string: a large nut, a small rubber ball. Bright colours track
  best; stop a nut from spinning on the string if you can.
- A fixed pivot that does not move (a hook, a nail, the edge of a shelf), visible in the frame.
- Measure **from the pivot to the centre of the bob** and say that length. On a 30 cm string,
  5 mm changes g by 1.7 %, so longer strings (50–100 cm) are more forgiving.
- Pull the bob aside **less than 15°** and release it without pushing. It must swing parallel
  to the wall, not towards the camera.
- Record **at least 10 swings**. 30 or 60 fps is fine.
- For a series, record the same pendulum at three or more lengths (for example 30, 40 and
  50 cm), then create a series in the app.

## Free fall (needs slow motion and a ruler)

Free fall needs a spatial scale and a fast camera. Without slow motion the ball blurs and only
10–25 frames cover the fall.

- Slow motion (120 or 240 fps) if the phone has it, otherwise the highest frame rate.
- A ruler or tape of known length taped to the wall **directly behind the path of the ball**,
  both ends visible, and say its length: *"Penggarisnya tiga puluh senti."*
- Measure the ball's diameter (circumference ÷ π with a string is easiest) and say it. With a
  ruler and a diameter FrameLab has two scales and gate G2 checks that they agree.
- Release the ball 1–2 cm from the wall, next to the ruler, without throwing it. The release
  point and the floor must both be in the frame.
- If the phone saves slow motion as a normal 30 fps file, choose the real capture rate in the
  upload form.

## A recording that should be refused (the trap)

Same set-up as free fall, but the ruler stands on a box about 50 cm in front of the wall,
closer to the camera than the ball. The ruler and the ball's own size then give two scales that
disagree, and FrameLab asks for a new recording (gate G2, "ruler off-plane"). Without a ruler
of known length there is only one scale and nothing to compare, so this check cannot work.
